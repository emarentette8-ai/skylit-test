Event study · QQQ, SPY, SPXW + 47 single names · Jul 1 – Sep 30, 2026

Do option strikes move price when it reaches them?

We tracked every time price entered a narrow band around a listed option strike, kept the approaches that failed to cross, and asked whether Skylit's gamma map at that moment helped forecast the next 15 minutes better than price behaviour and simpler gamma measures already did.

What the data could and could not support

The study ran on what exists in this project. Several parts of the brief need data that is not here, so those parts are restricted rather than approximated with invented numbers.

  • No NQ or ES futures. Outcomes are measured on the source underlying itself (QQQ primary; SPY and SPX index for SPXW as replications). Nothing here is futures hedge demand or a mapped overlay.
  • No option chains, trades, quotes, IV, OI or dealer inventory. The only positioning input is Skylit's per-strike net gamma, already netted across the nearest five expirations and both rights. Finite-shock hedge demand cannot be repriced; call/put and expiry splits (H2) and options flow (M4, H4) are blocked.
  • No volume or order book. The "observed market pressure" layer cannot be measured. Pressure is inferred from price only: returns, excursions, barrier hits, range and realised volatility.
  • Units and sign of net_gamma are not documented in the files, and the Skylit API docs were unreachable from this environment. The quantity is labelled vendor-estimated exposure throughout. A volatility check (below) supports the dealer-long-gamma reading for single names but not for QQQ.
  • 30-minute snapshots from 10:00 to 15:00 ET. Events before 10:00 are excluded from every GEX comparison; after 15:00 the last snapshot is up to 59 minutes old (recorded as f_snap_age).

What a trader learns: the run-up into the band is the same at real strikes and at arbitrary mid-strike levels, and so is what follows. Price arrives with momentum and keeps a small, statistically weak drift in the same direction, but this happens at any level of the same spacing. The strike itself does not change the path.

Time of day (0DTE clock proxy)

Real minus placebo difference in direction-adjusted 15-minute return by decision hour. The data cannot separate expirations, so minutes-to-close stands in for 0DTE time remaining. Labels show real-strike event counts.

What a trader learns: no hour shows a reliable strike effect, including the final hour when 0DTE gamma is largest. The 15:00 bucket is wide because it has the fewest events and the stalest snapshot.

Net vs gross gamma near the strike

Real-strike entries grouped by how much the strikes within ±0.25% offset each other: |net| ÷ gross. Low values mean large positive and negative exposures cancel. Bars: mean direction-adjusted 15-minute return, with 95% intervals. The call/put split is not in the data, so this is cancellation across nearby strikes, not between rights.

What a trader learns: a region whose net gamma is near zero because big exposures cancel behaves no differently from a one-sided region. None of the bars is reliably different from zero, and they are not reliably different from each other.

Example events (QQQ, fixed selection rule)

Rule, set before looking: September holdout days #1, #8 and #15 in date order; on each, the first eligible real-strike entry at or after 11:00 ET. Line: 1-minute closes. Band: the strike ± b. Vertical rule: decision time (UTC shown).

What a trader learns: typical events include stalls inside the band, quiet drifts back and occasional clean pushes through. The rule picked two non-crossings and one crossing; nothing about these three was chosen for how they turned out.

Secondary hypotheses

H1, timing. Within each entry episode we re-sampled the decision every minute and asked whether having crossed the strike predicts the next 15 minutes, beyond a flexible distance-to-strike term and minutes since entry. The crossing coefficient (direction-adjusted % return) is below. A real effect would show at real strikes and not at placebo levels.

H2, H3, H5: group contrasts. Differences in mean outcome between groups of real-strike entries, with day-block intervals and Holm-adjusted p-values within each family. Direction (15-minute return) and volatility (15-minute realised vol) are kept separate.

Are the volatility differences about strikes, or about when they happen? The same contrasts with and without pre-event controls (30-minute realised vol and range, absolute day return, time-of-day bins).

Sign-convention check. If positive vendor net gamma means dealers are long gamma, volatility after the event should be lower when aggregate net gamma is positive.

Robustness and practical value

The primary M3 vs M2 holdout comparison under the pre-declared sensitivities: alternative band widths, a snapshot one step (30 minutes) staler, and a 2-minute execution delay.

Trading rule. Trade in the forecast direction when the forecast is in the top 30% by size (threshold frozen on August), hold 15 minutes and pay a round-trip cost. The M3 rule is compared with the same rule built on price alone (M0).

Mechanics checks and review

These are logic checks on the hedging definitions and the event code, kept separate from the market results (tests/, 12 passing):

  • Long calls and long puts both have positive gamma; flipping the position sign reverses the modeled hedge adjustment (buy-on-rise for dealer short gamma, sell-on-rise for long).
  • A long call plus a short put at the same strike and expiry (a synthetic forward) has zero gamma. Moving it from OTM through ATM to ITM creates zero rehedging demand.
  • Relabeling a fixed position from OTM to ITM creates no new position; the hedge changes continuously through the strike, with no jump.
  • Zero inventory gives zero demand; multipliers scale linearly; finite hedge demand equals the integral of gamma over the price path; net and gross differ when components offset.
  • One same-strike crossing produces exactly one event; a jump across the whole band is classified separately; the 5-minute cooldown is enforced.
  • No look-ahead: for sampled QQQ and SPXW events, every feature recomputed from data truncated at the decision time is identical to the stored value. The outcome window starts at the next bar's open.

Single most useful next test

Get per-contract open interest and IV, split by call/put and expiry, at the same strikes and snapshot times (or the Skylit matrix layout rather than the netted view), plus NQ 1-minute bars. That would unblock the parts of this study that are currently missing: the call/put and expiry split (H2), actual finite-shock hedge demand (M3 as specified) and the futures outcome. Without those inputs, a positioning measure built from this netted per-strike map is a reshaping of local GEX, and local GEX added nothing here.

Appendix A — Exact specification
signed distance x_t = 100 · (S_t − K) / S_t (S_t = 1-min close) ATM band |x_t| ≤ b b = 0.05 pp (QQQ, SPY, singles); 0.025 pp (SPXW, strikes 0.065% apart) arming observed close with |x| ≥ 2b; re-arm ≥ 5 min after the prior entry at that strike entry (A) first close with |x| ≤ b while armed; s = +1 if armed from below, −1 from above (frozen) band jump armed, next close beyond the band on the opposite side → classified separately decision time τ = entry bar label + 1 min (bar closes are observable at label + 1) executable entry P0 = open of bar τ gamma snapshot latest Skylit snapshot with time ≤ τ (README: snapshot = latest at or before time) primary outcome R15 = s · 100 · (close of bar τ+14 − P0) / P0 (censored if τ+14 > 15:59) barrier ±0.10% from P0, 15-min deadline; both in one bar → "ambiguous", never resolved favourably crossing first close with s·x > 0 after τ, only for entries still on the approach side (s·x < 0) placebo levels midpoints between consecutive listed strikes, same state machine and days splits dev Jul 1–31 · validation Aug 1–31 · holdout Sep 1–30 (whole days; intraday labels → no purge gap) inference trading-day block bootstrap (2,000 reps); model comparisons on identical rows models ridge (penalty chosen on validation, refit dev+val, scored once on holdout); GBM sensitivity

Post-review change: the ridge penalty grid was extended from 1e5 to 1e7 after the reviewer showed that QQQ's choice sat at the old edge. The primary estimate moved from +0.01% to 0.00%. The residual-increment test (M3-only features fitted to M2's errors, each penalty chosen on August) was added as the direct test of the M3 increment.

Model feature sets. M0: approach direction, distance, prior 1/5/15/30-min and day returns, 30-min realised vol and range, speed, acceleration, time of day (and squared), prior encounters at the strike, strike roundness. M1: + aggregate net/gross ratio, log gross, snapshot age, change in net ratio since the prior snapshot. M2: + local strike share, ±0.25% region net/gross/cancellation, Skylit node type, change in local share, distance×local and local×aggregate interactions. M3: + net gamma in shock windows ahead of and behind the strike (±0.10%, ±0.20%) and their asymmetry, king distance and sign, interactions. M4 (options flow) is not estimable.

Note on M3: QQQ and SPY strikes are about 0.13–0.14% apart, so the primary ±0.10% window rarely contains a second strike. With this data the primary-size shock feature mostly reduces to the local gamma already in M2; only the ±0.20% window carries new information.
